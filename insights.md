# Insights: Duka Smart Electronics

I analysed 800 orders from 150 customers (January 2025 to September 2026). Unless I say otherwise, money figures use Completed orders only and are in KSh. Total revenue came to 24,155,371.70 and total profit to 2,890,921.70, so the business keeps only about 12 cents of every shilling it sells.

## 1. Most of our revenue comes from about a quarter of our customers
**What I found:** In Q45 I grouped customers by what they spent. 34 customers spent KSh 250,000 or more, and together they brought in about KSh 16.97M. That is roughly 70% of all revenue. The 40 smallest buyers brought in only about KSh 0.75M, around 3%.
**Why it matters:** If a few of these 34 stop buying, sales will drop quickly.
**Recommendation:** Give these customers special attention: a named contact, first access to new stock and a loyalty discount. Review the list every month.

## 2. Accessories sell little but earn a lot per shilling
**What I found:** From Q33 and Q42, accessories are only 4.46% of revenue, yet their profit margin is 42.39%. The other four categories earn between about 10% and 11%. Accessories made KSh 456,746 profit, almost the same as home appliances (KSh 458,034), which sell nearly four times more.
**Why it matters:** With overall profit at about 12%, the high-margin products are what keep the business healthy.
**Recommendation:** Sell accessories together with phones and laptops (a charger or earbuds at checkout) and avoid deep discounts on them.

## 3. Sales are growing, but January falls off a cliff
**What I found:** In Q31 the three best months are all in 2026: August (KSh 2.24M), September (KSh 2.09M) and June (KSh 2.04M). Q40 shows January 2026 dropped 84% from December, to KSh 322,433, the lowest month in the data (Q46d). February then jumped 136%.
**Why it matters:** The business has to carry costs through a very quiet January.
**Recommendation:** Build up stock for December, and plan an offer for January to keep sales moving.

## 4. A third of customers who bought have stopped
**What I found:** In Q34, 47 customers have not completed an order in more than 90 days. One has been quiet for 625 days. Q23 also shows 12 customers who have never ordered, and 6 more who ordered but never completed a purchase.
**Why it matters:** These people already know the shop. Winning them back costs less than finding new customers.
**Recommendation:** Send the 47 a win-back message with an offer, and follow up with the 12 who never ordered to find out what stopped them.

## 5. Nairobi carries the business, and other towns are thin
**What I found:** Q25 shows Nairobi brings in KSh 9.58M, about 40% of revenue, with 48 of the 150 customers. Machakos has only 3 customers and KSh 0.75M (Q17).
**Why it matters:** One city holds a big share of the business, and smaller towns are barely reached.
**Recommendation:** Try a delivery offer or local promotion in the weakest towns, starting with Machakos, and see whether customer numbers grow.

## 6. VIP customers spend much more than Retail customers
**What I found:** In Q27 the average VIP customer spent about KSh 253,000, Corporate about KSh 198,000 and Retail about KSh 164,000. Retail is also the biggest group, with 88 buyers.
**Why it matters:** Moving Retail customers up even slightly would add a lot of revenue.
**Recommendation:** Set a clear spending target for VIP status and show Retail customers how close they are.

## 7. One in nine orders is cancelled
**What I found:** Q20 shows 11.25% of orders (90 out of 800) were cancelled.
**Why it matters:** Cancelled orders waste staff time and may point to stock or payment problems.
**Recommendation:** Record why orders are cancelled (stock, payment, delivery time) and fix the most common reason first.
